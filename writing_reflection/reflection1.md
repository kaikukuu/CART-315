2026-09-14 - Reflection 1 - The Stuff Games are Made Of Ch 2: Rules: Change Comes from Within

Having had Pippin himself as my prof for a creative computing class it was interesting to read his words and how much it aligns with my experience. when Pippin dicusses the purpose of art in games it resonated with me. As an artist first and comp sci student second I've always wanted to find ways of incorporating art and creativity into programming, often by using materials that don't seem related to computing to represent computational concepts in a distinct way. I admire simple concepts and the people who can stick to working on a project. When the game begins to feel banale I always want to get straight into a final product. This also reflects other computing/programming best practices to make the simplest version of your project and make it work well before you create an unmanageable mess of bloated unrefined code that make it difficult to fix bugs. Once further assets are gathered it may be incredibly difficult to backtrack when encountering issues. However... I have personally struggled with planning and how much or how little is best. This makes me not want to start anything which is the worst feeling as a creative. 

Returning to Pong, what makes it fun? The way people use it to interact with one another and the game world? I found it interesting to experiement and how Pippin demonstrated how putting a spin on the original yields nearly infinite possibilies (if that's what you really wanted to do.) It works because poeple would likely be familiar so they can draw on that previous knowledge to make inferences to adapt to new versions. These simple changes to the mechanics/appearance of the game can invoke such varying emotions despite its simplicity. This can be used in a broader way. A little line change can so drastically change the strategy of the game even though the basic concept remains. Unfair pong stood out to me as I can imagine the feeling of cheering for the underdog due to the perception of the game players being on unequal footing. By prompting a player to reflect on human experiences and beating the odds we can't help but want to support the player with less resources.

I also really relate to the paragraph about accidentally figuring out what you thought was a bug could become a little feature (Fertility Pong) that began as a mistake. The idea of not always needing to optimize everythig about a game and that like in real life there are little quirks everywjere caused by "mistakes" resonates with me too. For instance for living beings random mistakes in DNA transcription is what has facilitated evolution and diversification, along with making each of us unique. The mistakes I've made always inform any future attempts and has often led me to epiphanies about new ideas or improvements. The statement that will remain in my toolbox of skills is to be "in conversation with creation."
