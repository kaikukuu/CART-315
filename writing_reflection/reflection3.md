
I find the study for a anatomy of a prototype very interesting. I haven’t thought about how exactly to define a prototype or a more concrete description of how prototypes should be conceptualized. The paper’s studies is very enlightening in providing separate approaches in the prototyping and their use cases: difference between a prototype/prototyping in the contexts of filtering or manifestations of ideas.Specifically it looks at this in the field of HCL and design, identifying where certain existing protyping practices fail, especially with the flexibility of the concepts and possibilities. Most conversations and previous definitions for prototyping were very concerned with levels of fidelity rather than other concepts exploring design spaces. I was confused between the phrases: fundamental principle vs economic principle. For some reason I kept thinking of them as the same and I'm not sure why? Since I learned about prototyping and heuristics in a few previous classes, it was always hammered in that we need to start with low fidelity, be it a wireframe represensation of an app (digital) or testing simple physical designs with materials. I kind of thought of prototypes as different design steps leading linearly to a final product. I didn't really think about how when prototyping I could test specific aspects by filtering those aspects through different forms without other functions distracting from the one I want to test. It would allow allow the focus to be on one aspect I choose instead of having to deal with all the aspects at the same time. I found the section on Filtering dimensions really sparked my curiousity expecially the example given of the differences in interpretations based on the format of a prototype. One example discusses mainly two groups looking at the same idea but in a different prototpe and how the groups interpretation of the space they were shown (2D room plan vs 3D First person VR). When asked follow up questions despite the room being the same room they were described very differently by participants. There was also the figure showing the different filters used when prototyping a camcorder that was pretty fascinating to me. It's obviously not a new concept but it is a new way for me to better understand the prototyping process as I tend to too detail-oriented from the jump. When designing I go into this mode of trying to organize every little aspect all at the same time because I'm afraid I'll somehow forget all that I want to implement by the time I get there. The issue with this is that firstly, I get utterly overwhelmed by the amount of details while I also forget that designing is a reflective process that can start super basic while informing the next steps. I can't always accurately predict how certain materials may actually end up interacting so working on smaller parts individually in order to test them seems like a good step in my design process.
