2026-11-01

In my initial brainstorm I was inspired by some of my previous game projects: one being a 2D game set at an observatory where the player has to match constellations in the sky; the other which is 3D representation of the fragments of the room and objects in my memory from what I used to see when visiting my grandma as a kid. The room is illuminated and set within a dark universe full of stars as a reference to the dead returning to the stars and the memories taken with them. This is all to say, I have a bit of an interest in outer space and its entities but not exactly in a sci-fi way? More in a representational way such as the imagery of birth and rebirth, along with the mystical nature of space and coming to terms with the imposing nature of being but a speck in the grand scheme of things. Concept: it's dream-like in the way that it's strange. I was also thinking about all the different planets from Le Petit Prince which was too ambitious but I could maybe just take inspiration. 

The initial walking simulator was intended to be some kind of walk thru space/the stars and creating a space that has an unnerving yet etherial feeling to it. I had no idea how to even start and what to build in Unity since I wasn't sure what the best course of actions was. I had imagined perhaps using particle logic to recreate a dark sky of stars but then decided just to start by trying to create my own star assets so I could have more control over them and simpler for me to understand with the timeframe. I imagined different ways to make the stars be more than just a still circle such as using light emitting materials, twinkling and different possible movements, and sounds. Initially I liked the idea of making the stars sound like wind chimes and having the typical ambient sound. While using the 3D spheres for the stars I also thought about other interactions that I could make while using only that one shape. One of these was possibly having a blackhole corner, which ended up leading to the free roaming blackhole you see in the most recent version of the prototype that sucks up stars and resets the game if it comes in contact with the player. 

That was the point I had reached when presenting my prototype in class so after this I wanted to make the game play be more interesting for players. So I started experimenting with Terrain objects and experimenting with the different brushes to build up structures. As for the lighting I really enjoyed the non-shaded look on 3D objects so the appearance of the prototype is more of an asthetics choice although I still don't quite understand how it all works. I wanted to use the terrain object to paint back on some mountains for the player to walk through and explore but using the terrain made the map so massive that no matter what I was trying to add it looked tiny. This left the horizon very barren which I liked but I don't know if it would be interesting for someone to actually explore? I then kept thinking about how I could get the player to float in order to reach stars higher up which I ended up solving by creating a platform that the player can ride and has separate controls (maybe only for now). As well as some kind of pathway or ramp to be able to have somewhere safe from the roaming blackhole. Then as I was testing the player view I kept running right off the edges so I incorporated invisible walls around the edges of the plane (for some reason sometime they haven't worked and i have no clue why). Made a big star that goes in a random path leaving stars behind it-> still only produces star1 objects. Similarly I implemented the blackhole that goes in a random path and sucks up stars; if the player comes into contact with it the scene is reset as a kind of challenge or goal to avoid. 

Given that the prototype is due today this session I attempted to pull the walking sim together but I still have no idea whether it did come together. I got really frustrated yesterday as I made negligible progress and I've been finding it a bit difficult to navigate and reorder 3d objects and scenes while learning the unity controls which has been a bit discouraging. In the ended I used the stairs and stair landing assets from the example playroom which gave me something to work with and birthed the current form of the rising steps and long pathways. I know it may look lazy but legitemately I like the look of the orange color with the rest of the color scheme so I didn't change the material. My last courses of action were inspired by the sounds as I was scouting, like the heartbeat idea for the red star came from me hearing a sound clip that had a beat similar sounding to that of a heart and I was stuck between using a vacuuming sound or strange sound for the blackhole. Finally I added stars to the ramps to direct/entice a player to explore that way and the platform serves as more of a way to more closely approach the red star and just examine the scene from above although I thought it might end up becoming an elevator (not implemented).

P2 JOURNAL 
2026-10-22

For some reason I really had a difficult time starting off this second prototype. My initial problem was that I had been really sick over the reading week which left me exhausted and incredibly uncreative so I kept putting off working on it. I kept thinking "I'll start when I feel inspired" but that moment never really hit. Finally, I was out of time and despite pages of brainstorming I knew I just had to start actually trying different paths. While exploring the three initial games and playing through them to observe their behaviour I was particularily caught by the appearance of the bricks in the brick breaker game. It reminded me first of a kids xylophone initially so I thought about adapting the game to be musical in some way. The idea began as as game where the player is shown specific brick patterns and have to try to hit the bricks in the correct order and beat by bouncing the ball off the paddle. however I found this to be far too challenging and I got stuck again. So I began to wrack my brain for any solutions. What I ended up coming up with was that I would completely get rid of the ball, paddle and their collision logic. I exhange it for control buttons for the player to select a column and the color indicates how many times the column button needs to be pressed. This choice was inspired by the maps provided in the rythmn game Quaver: https://store.steampowered.com/app/980610/Quaver/ and "la piece de resistance", the Simon Swipe Game Device by Hasbro: https://instructions.hasbro.com/en-ca/instruction/simon-swipe-game  
Demo of inspo game: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcnlVGqEoj8


At first I wanted the tiles light up in patterns and player must bounce ball off the right bricks shown in the order but as I said this was too upredictable and I was having issues with manipulating the brick sprites colors. and I couldn't get the ball hitting the paddle angles to be consistent enough to be able to make any game progress as a player so I initially tried to research splines and putting the paddle on a curved path then trying to see if I could curve the paddle since I wanted to mimic the circle imagery of the Simon Swipe. Turns out there was a pretty simple solution to not being able to hightlight bricks: just to toggle them and indicating which button the player is selecting by making the button change size to indicate being selected/unselected. 


I felt so stuck at the beginning and had so much on my mind that I didn't have the mindspace to come up with a topic that i felt would be succesful and mesh well with the base game for the brick breaking game. This whole prototype has been a challenge and I ended up nearly gutting all of the brick breaker base game. This wasn't the best idea when you're running low on time... My next steps were to have fewer bricks so remembering a pattern won't be too overwhelming from the start. I wanted to keep the lvls having their own maps but wanted to make different patterns as sub levels.
One big thing I needed to figure out was how I would format the brick light patterns so they can be easily displayed and used to validate the player's response. I outlined what would be in the first level as the following:

lvl1:

blue(b) brick: 1 click
gree(g): 2 clicks
yellow: 3 clicks

experiemental patterns: 
1.1: b1,b2,b3,b4,b5
1.2: b1,b2,b3,b4,b5,g1,g2,g3,g4,g5

playthru logic for game:
-> the tiles all light up once in a pattern
-> when the pattern is done it's the player's turn to repeat the pattern using the arrows L-R to move to the correct column's button then repeat the pattern by pressing the correct number of times for the tile's color and in the correct order 

-Remove: ball, paddle and resetzone
brick.cs: edit or maybe discard most, bricks will be separate from buttons, logic for lighting up patterns buttons.cs

My remaining thoughts include how can I change the rest of the game code to change this from a brick breaker game to a "simon-says" pattern call and response game. the unbreakable "bricks" are now buttons that the player uses to repeat back the pattern displayed on the bricks at the start of each sublvl. The player uses the right and left keys to switch between the buttons (columns) and press the space bar the correct number of times according to its color. I want the bricks to stay up in the same positions throughout lvl 1 then updates at lvl 2. The sublvls should use the same map but different patterns that escalate in difficulty. any logic from the brick breaker that wouldn't be useful can be overriden. In the end I really wish I had tried to jump in and just experiment sooner since I do like the concept I ended up coming up with for this prototype. It would have been nice to have given myself some more time to properly accomplish my goals and better demonstrate my ideas. Unfortunately, being ill took a big toll and I should have been better prepared. Good thing is that I hope not to put myself in this position going forward.

P1 PONG JOURNAL
2026-19-30 10:30PM

Reflecting back on my brainstorm and initial ideas the options I came up with were leaning towards the concepts of either Disguised or Interference Pong as they are the simplest to implement. I didn't want to get too carried away and end up not completing the task so I'm attempting to rein myself in during the ideation phase. I began developing/implementing what I call Interference Pong. The basic idea was to recreate a kind of randomness that adds excitement and surprise from outside forces. I added many methods to control the different types of interference by messing with the speed of the ball, the paddle controls, points controlled by timers. I may have gone a bit overboard but I do feel like I've reached a solid point where I've built the base of what I imagined for Interference Pong. My focus was on the features of swapping the direction of moving objects, resetting objects/score, reversing the logic of the paddles for both player and CPU. To manage all the possible events I made driver class called the InterferenceManager controlled by a randomized timer which restarts at the beginning of each round and uses a random range to choose which case of interference will occur. After replaying my game with my updates I added yesterday with the freezing paddles I realized I'd just left the placeholder methods which was why the console would notify that an effect was implemented but the paddles still move. Anyway, I also realized I hadn't thought about having a game loop with a start and end screen. I also sort of implemented something that prevents the ball from getting stuck in the horizontal motion but I want to actually want to make a proper fix for that. After my initial base game while play testing my changes I decided that the game play was a bit boring so I separated the paddle interferece events from the ones affecting the ball and the scores. This allowed me to layer the events which allowed the game to be more interesting and lively as a ball/score event can co-occur with a paddle event. 

At the end of my inital version I thought I was already close to being at the point where I no longer needed to focus on the gameplay and could possibly explore some different appearance changes, provided I have the time and don't run into too many bugs. However, I did end up running out of energy to make visual change but I believe Interference Pong's concept and implementation is already strong. I made a lot of finishing touches to Interference Pong and it seems to have come together well. It's pretty much still my introduction to Unity and writing in C#, although I have lots of experience in other OOP languages like Java and C++ which informs my choices in how I decided to design the methods and Inteference Manager. I keep getting the itch to delve further in customizating assets and the appearance of the game but I'm holding myself back in order to focus on the implementation of features first. I love to be creative with visuals but I really want to challenge myself to start from the foundation (the program) rather than the appearance (assets and visual design). I tend to get ahead of myself when I'm excited and I delve into complex projects too quickly not taking the time to plan. This project has eessentially been an exercise in ignoring my need to make everything super nice looking and polished (aka my perfectionism rearing its head). By submitting this project in the state it is currently in wihout many outward chnages it highlights the gameplay changes not visible until the game is played and experienced. Were I to continue the next mechanic I wanted to implement in order to make the game more interesting and challenging is the idea of negative point balls. The way I started conceptualizing this was that it would be included in the interference so at the start of a new round there could spawn a few very fast moving red balls that would make the player have to quickly invert their thinking as hitting them with the paddle will lose you a point. I wasn't exactly sure whether I wanted the negative balls to treat the goals as invisible then despawn or maybe use a timer to create a sort of final "fever time" where the player who gets hit first loses the whole game.
