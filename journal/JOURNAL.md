2026-19-30 10:30PM

Reflecting back on my brainstorm and initial ideas the options I came up with were leaning towards the concepts of either Disguised or Interference Pong as they are the simplest to implement. I didn't want to get too carried away and end up not completing the task so I'm attempting to rein myself in during the ideation phase. I began developing/implementing what I call Interference Pong. The basic idea was to recreate a kind of randomness that adds excitement and surprise from outside forces. I added many methods to control the different types of interference by messing with the speed of the ball, the paddle controls, points controlled by timers. I may have gone a bit overboard but I do feel like I've reached a solid point where I've built the base of what I imagined for Interference Pong. My focus was on the features of swapping the direction of moving objects, resetting objects/score, reversing the logic of the paddles for both player and CPU. To manage all the possible events I made driver class called the InterferenceManager controlled by a randomized timer which restarts at the beginning of each round and uses a random range to choose which case of interference will occur. After replaying my game with my updates I added yesterday with the freezing paddles I realized I'd just left the placeholder methods which was why the console would notify that an effect was implemented but the paddles still move. Anyway, I also realized I hadn't thought about having a game loop with a start and end screen. I also sort of implemented something that prevents the ball from getting stuck in the horizontal motion but I want to actually want to make a proper fix for that. After my initial base game while play testing my changes I decided that the game play was a bit boring so I separated the paddle interferece events from the ones affecting the ball and the scores. This allowed me to layer the events which allowed the game to be more interesting and lively as a ball/score event can co-occur with a paddle event. 

At the end of my inital version I thought I was already close to being at the point where I no longer needed to focus on the gameplay and could possibly explore some different appearance changes, provided I have the time and don't run into too many bugs. However, I did end up running out of energy to make visual change but I believe Interference Pong's concept and implementation is already strong. I made a lot of finishing touches to Interference Pong and it seems to have come together well. It's pretty much still my introduction to Unity and writing in C#, although I have lots of experience in other OOP languages like Java and C++ which informs my choices in how I decided to design the methods and Inteference Manager. I keep getting the itch to delve further in customizating assets and the appearance of the game but I'm holding myself back in order to focus on the implementation of features first. I love to be creative with visuals but I really want to challenge myself to start from the foundation (the program) rather than the appearance (assets and visual design). I tend to get ahead of myself when I'm excited and I delve into complex projects too quickly not taking the time to plan. This project has eessentially been an exercise in ignoring my need to make everything super nice looking and polished (aka my perfectionism rearing its head). By submitting this project in the state it is currently in wihout many outward chnages it highlights the gameplay changes not visible until the game is played and experienced. Were I to continue the next mechanic I wanted to implement in order to make the game more interesting and challenging is the idea of negative point balls. The way I started conceptualizing this was that it would be included in the interference so at the start of a new round there could spawn a few very fast moving red balls that would make the player have to quickly invert their thinking as hitting them with the paddle will lose you a point. I wasn't exactly sure whether I wanted the negative balls to treat the goals as invisible then despawn or maybe use a timer to create a sort of final "fever time" where the player who gets hit first loses the whole game.
